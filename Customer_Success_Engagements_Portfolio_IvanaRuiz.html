01 / Approach
Ivana Ruiz / The idea behind the work

Ivana's Coaching Feedback for MTA and NYCT customer engagements.

I used real MTA and NYCT customer conversations as coaching material, examining the rider's underlying need, the agent's response, and what made that response either effective or less helpful. The result was a practical language for improving tone, clarity and service quality across a customer facing team.

Case study / More or Less Like This

A brand voice is only as strong as the conversations that bring it to life.

Working across customer service and digital communications, I framed actual engagements as learning opportunities: what to reinforce, what to refine, and how to stay useful, empathetic and accurate under pressure.

Ivana Ruiz / Engagement gallery and coaching feedback

Ivana's Coaching Feedback: more like this, less like this.

Real MTA and NYCT customer posts and agent responses reveal the difference between a technically correct reply and a response that makes the customer feel understood. Each example includes the coaching principle I drew from the interaction.

Show examples
More like thisCLARITY / 01

Explain the actual service change.

Customer post

“Another complete @MTA fail. F train stops at Jay, no explanation as to what to do.”

Agent response / excerpt

“Tonight there is planned work on the F line. It is arriving on the A line track at Jay St, and then running along A line to Hoyt St.”

✓
Why it works

Specific, usable information gives the customer a way forward. Helpful answers address the decision someone needs to make/not just the fact that a disruption exists.

More like thisACCOUNTABILITY / 02

Own the experience without defensiveness.

Customer post

“I think the statute of limitations on the @MTA blaming this situation on Sandy has expired.”

Agent response / excerpt

“We're not trying to make excuses. We take full accountability and are willing to do the work to fix things.”

✓
Why it works

Acknowledgment and ownership shift the conversation from an argument about blame to a credible commitment to improve.

More like thisFOLLOW-THROUGH / 03

Connect empathy to a concrete action.

Customer post

“Car 8785 Queens bound N - speakers so low and muffled can't hear or understand any announcements.”

Agent response

“Hi, Mark. We know how frustrating this can be. We’ll report this and get car 8785 scheduled for maintenance. Thanks for your reporting.”

✓
Why it works

The response validates the impact of the issue, uses the customer's specific report, and tells the customer what will happen next.

More like thisEXPERTISE / 04

Make knowledge useful to the customer.

Customer post / excerpt

“Every day around 7:20 AM there is a not in service 7 train on middle tracks approaching Flushing. Why?”

Agent response

“Hi Kim. The trains you've observed are empty trains coming from the storage yard. They will typically proceed to the terminal at Main Street and be put into passenger service.”

✓
Why it works

Operational knowledge addresses the real question, resolves ambiguity, and builds confidence through an informative answer.

Less like thisRELEVANCE / 05

Listen to what was already provided.

Customer post / excerpt

“Why was this new countdown clock on the s/b Canal St NQ station placed directly in front [of] big metal CCTV screens?”

Agent response / excerpt

“Can you tell us if this is the Brooklyn-bound platform? Also, download our http://SubwayTime.mta.info to your device.”

↗
Coaching opportunity

The customer had already supplied the direction of travel. A better reply would acknowledge the obstruction and focus on the concern rather than suggesting an unrelated tool.

Less like thisSPECIFICITY / 06

Replace vague assurances with the next step.

Customer post

“Air conditioning is broken on the 1 train car #2173 #CuomosMTA @NYCTSubway @NYGovCuomo”

Agent response

“Hi- We will look into it. Thanks for letting us know.”

↗
Coaching opportunity

When possible, describe what will happen to the car report and who will review it. Specificity can reassure without promising a repair timeline that is not known.

Less like thisCUSTOMER NEED / 07

Answer the question behind the complaint.

Customer post / excerpt

“What is happening with the Far Rockaway trains? No shuttle buses, nothing. We get off at Howard Beach to hear nothing.”

Agent response / excerpt

“We had a suspension in A train service between Howard Beach and Far Rockaway in both directions because of switch problems at Broad Channel, service has being restored.”

↗
Coaching opportunity

The customer needed useful guidance about service alternatives and communications. Explaining an incident that had already cleared did not fully address those needs.

Less like thisSERVICE RECOVERY / 08

Tell the customer why you're asking.

Customer post / excerpt

“Just saw a guy take a piss in the staircase at Bedford-Nostrand station.”

Agent response

“Good afternoon, Jimmy. That's awful. Do you have the location of the staircase where this incident occurred?”

↗
Coaching opportunity

The clarifying question is useful. Explaining that the location would help request cleaning connects the question to a meaningful customer outcome.

The operational connection

Customer engagement is a workflow.

In the transit environment, digital service conversations were handled alongside correspondence and CRM processes. Customer comments, commendations and actionable complaints could be tagged, routed and tracked, linking the front line response to the teams responsible for follow through.

  • Public conversation and customer correspondence
  • Social relationship management and customer tracking
  • Cross-functional routing and issue resolution
  • Feedback-informed service improvement
The capability connection

Skills that travel across industries.

These capabilities apply to customer success, client experience, service operations and team enablement, whether the channel is social media, email, support software or an enterprise customer relationship.
